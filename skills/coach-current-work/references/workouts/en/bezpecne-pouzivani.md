# Find Out What You Can Input into AI: The Rule for Your Account

AI searches your account terms to find out what happens to the data you input and saves a brief rule from it.

## Find Out What You Can Input into AI: The Rule for Your Account

**Difficulty: easy.**\
**Time: approximately 15 minutes** with one account and one material.
## What It’s For

On Thursday, you send an offer to Novák Stavby and want AI to shorten it to one page. At work, you have corporate AI; at home, a personal account where you write better. Can you input the offer with the client’s name and price into your personal account?

It depends on the account: personal accounts often allow conversations to be used for model training unless you disable it in settings, while corporate accounts are governed by your company’s contract with the provider. Therefore, AI will look up the rules exactly for your account.
## What to Prepare

- An AI account where you don’t know what you can input, personal or corporate,
- Your company’s AI rules and client contract; do not input these fully into AI,
- One real material for this week: an offer, spreadsheet, or email,
- A chat with AI that can search the web.
## How to Proceed

1. Insert the prompt from the AI exercise into the AI for which you want the rule and answer its questions. AI will look up the provider’s terms to see what happens with your data and show the rule.
2. Correct anything that doesn’t fit, find one citation via the link and Ctrl+F. Write “agreed” and AI will save the rule or tell you where. In a new chat, ask it what rule it has.
3. Describe the material and the data in it to AI. According to the rule, it will tell you what to replace, for example, “Client A” instead of the company name, and then work with it.

Company rules and client contracts take precedence over provider terms.
## AI Exercise

Insert this [prompt](glossary:prompt) into the AI you use at work:

Find out what I am allowed to input into this AI account and save a rule you will follow in future chats.

First, ask me what tool and plan I use, for example, personal ChatGPT Plus or Microsoft Copilot with a work account, whether the account was provided by my company, and what my company’s AI rules or client contract say. If I don’t know the plan, tell me where to find it in settings. It’s enough if I summarize the company rules in my own words.

Then research the official terms and provider help for exactly this plan:
- whether my conversations and files are used for model training and where to disable it,
- how long conversations are stored and who has access,
- whether my company’s contract with the provider applies to the account.
For each finding, provide a link, the date you read the page, and a short exact quote in the page’s language. Do not guess what you don’t find.

From this, write a rule for this account, no more than ten lines, in three parts:
- Allowed: what I can input without changes.
- Replace: what I replace with placeholders before input, e.g., client names with “Client A,” and personal data not needed for the task.
- Never: passwords, access codes, credit card numbers, and everything forbidden by company rules.
Also include health, religion, and union membership data in Never unless the company explicitly allows them for this account. Company rules and client contracts override provider permissions. Anything you can’t verify, write as a question for a specific person, e.g., IT administrator or client. Put source links with dates below the rule.

Show me the rule, incorporate my corrections, and save it in memory when I say it’s agreed. If you have no memory, shorten it to fit into Custom Instructions and tell me where to find them in settings. If you work with folders on a computer, save the rule in your rules applying to all folders, not just one project.

Finally, ask me what material I want to work with today and what data it contains, without the data itself yet. According to the rule, tell me if I can input it fully or what to replace before input. After I input the material, do the work I need.

## In Your Tool

### Microsoft Copilot
With a work account, corporate data protection applies in Copilot: Microsoft does not use queries, answers, or corporate data for model training and searches the web unless the company disables it. When you write that the rule is agreed, it saves it to memory; if not, write “remember this rule.” You create a scheduled task with a Microsoft Copilot license via Schedule this prompt on the sent prompt.

### ChatGPT
Personal ChatGPT accounts may use conversations for training until you disable Improve the model for everyone under Data controls in Settings; Business and Enterprise do not use them by default. AI saves the rule in memory; if you don’t have memory, add it under Custom Instructions. AI creates scheduled tasks on request; overview is under Scheduled.

### Claude
Personal Free, Pro, and Max accounts have a Privacy setting Model Improvement that controls whether Anthropic may use chats to improve models; Team and Enterprise do not use chats for training by default. AI saves the rule in memory. In Cowork, also keep it in Instructions for Claude in Settings.

### Gemini
With a Google Workspace work account, Google does not use your data for training without permission; personal accounts do and some chats are read by people, which you can disable in Gemini Apps Activity. Work accounts have no memory or custom instructions, so insert the rule into the Gem robot’s instructions (Roboti Gem, Nový Gem) and open a new chat there.
## If It Has Already Happened

If something that shouldn’t be sent to AI was, e.g., a customer export to a personal account, immediately do three things:

1. **Stop using the conversation and do not share it.** Do not delete it until told by the person you report to. Deletion does not undo what was sent, and the record is needed for assessment.
2. **Write down five facts:** what happened (type of data, not content), when it happened and when you discovered it, where it went (tool and account), who it may concern, and what you have done. Write unknowns as unknown.
3. **Report it immediately** to the person handling it in your company, e.g., IT or data protection officer. For client work, also report to the client contact. For personal data, the company often must notify the authority, preferably within 72 hours of learning about it.

AI will draft the report from the five facts. Do not input the original content again.
## Keep the Rule Up to Date

AI providers change terms and what they do with personal account data. Keep the rule current with a [scheduled task](card:recurring-ai-task) AI runs quarterly, which you can create anytime after the workout. Insert this instruction into the chat where you wrote the rule and ask AI to create the scheduled task. If your tool lacks scheduled tasks, set a calendar reminder and send the instruction yourself quarterly.

```text
Once a quarter, review the official terms and help for my plan. If anything affecting my data rule changed, tell me what changed with a link and date, and suggest a new rule version. If nothing changed, write just one sentence. When creating the task, include my exact plan name and the full current rule.
```
## Example

Jana coordinates projects at a construction company. At home, she writes in a personal AI account with a paid plan; at work, she has corporate AI. AI found in the terms that Jana’s plan uses conversations for training unless disabled in settings. Jana turned off the switch and told AI what the IT directive says: client materials only in corporate AI. AI showed her the rule; Jana added project numbers, and AI saved it in memory:

```text
Rule for my personal AI account, paid plan (verified 10/9/2026)
- Allowed: my own texts and parts of materials without client names and prices.
- Replace: client and company names (Client A, Company B), contacts, project numbers, and people’s names not needed for the task.
- Never: passwords, access codes, card numbers, health data, and entire client materials with prices. These belong in corporate AI (IT directive, point 3).
- Question for IT: may I input an internal price list without client names?
- Sources: provider help, article on data settings (10/9/2026); IT directive from 3/1/2026.
```

Jana wanted to rewrite the introduction of the offer for Novák Stavby in her personal account. AI told her according to the rule to replace “Novák Stavby” with “Client A” and omit the site manager’s name because the introduction doesn’t need it. Jana replaced two names, input the introduction, and AI rewrote it. She then shortened the entire offer with the price in corporate AI.
## If You Get Stuck

- **AI didn’t find terms or can’t search the web:** tell it the exact tool and plan name as shown in account settings. If web search is disabled in a corporate account by the admin, have AI write the rule from company directives and leave unverified points as questions for IT.
- **No company AI rules:** AI writes the rule based on provider terms and adds a question for your manager or IT at the end. Until answered, client names and materials belong in Replace.
- **New chat doesn’t know the rule:** if the rule is in project instructions, open a new chat directly in the project. Otherwise, memory is likely off or disabled by the admin in the corporate account. Add a shortened rule under your Custom Instructions, the field AI reads at each chat start. See the [Custom Instructions](card:custom-instructions) card for how.
- **Task without real names makes no sense:** do it in the AI where it belongs per the rule, e.g., corporate AI. If you want the finished text later in your personal account, have names replaced right away in corporate AI.
- **Material has some names the rule forbids:** replace them in a text editor or spreadsheet with Find and Replace, then input the material.
## What Will Be Ready

You have a rule for your AI account with sources and date. AI saved it, and you verified it in a new chat. According to it, you prepared the first real material, and AI did the work you needed. You also know what to do in the first minutes if something that shouldn’t be in AI was sent.

## Prompt

**What it is:** An instruction for a specific interaction with AI.

**What it is not:** It is not the same as long-term context, a stored assistant, or an agent.

**Why the student needs it:** It triggers the exercise and guides its progress; it is not the main learning content.

## Service Outage, Limit, or Overloaded Chat

A common fallback for operational interruptions when working with AI. It is accessible from any card via the **Outage or Exhausted Limit** input. It does not create its own card, check-in, or multiple copies of the same text.

The specific section **When Something Goes Wrong** in the card further addresses errors in input, resources, tools, or procedures. This mechanic triggers when the AI cannot technically continue in the current pass.

## First, distinguish what happened

| What you see | Likely situation | First step |
|---|---|---|
| Tool reports exhausted limit, too many requests, or a reset time | tariff or temporary operational limit | note the reset time and save a return point |
| AI forgets the beginning, refuses additional attachments, or reports input too long | context, file, or task too large | create a handoff and continue in a new context or more suitable environment |
| Tool does not respond, returns a server error, or multiple models fail | service outage | verify official status and choose an approved substitute or pause |

If the AI still responds, it may name the situation itself and prepare a return point. If it does not respond, skip this step. Repeatedly resubmitting the same request will not save the work state.

## Save a return point

Before waiting or switching elsewhere, preserve the smallest package from which you can safely continue:

```text
Goal: ...
What is done and where it is: ...
Confirmed decisions and constraints: ...
Used resources or files: ...
Nearest next step: ...
What should not be changed or recreated: ...
```

If the AI still responds, use:

> Prepare a concise return point for continuing in a new chat or tool. Preserve the goal, completed parts, confirmed decisions, resources, constraints, and one nearest next step. Do not invent anything not present in the conversation.

For longer work, save the return point in the project README or another approved workspace. Sensitive inputs remain only in approved storage.

## Tariff or operational limit reached

1. Find out when the limit resets and whether it applies to the model, function, or entire account.
2. Decide between pausing, reducing scope, or using an approved substitute model or environment.
3. When switching, transfer the return point and only the resources necessary for the next step.
4. Verify that the substitute can handle the required files, tools, and actions. A weaker model may suffice for formatting but fail in analysis or tool use.

## Context, file, or task is too large

1. Have the AI create a handoff before it loses earlier decisions if it still responds.
2. Start a new chat or project with the return point and links to canonical files.
3. Divide work according to meaningful units and preserve shared criteria for the outcome. Randomly splitting text may lose relationships between parts.
4. If the task requires multiple files, bulk work, or project changes, continue via the `when-chat-is-not-enough` card.

## Tool or model outage

1. Verify the provider’s official status. Address account errors only when the service is generally operational.
2. Save the return point outside the currently nonfunctional conversation if it is still accessible.
3. Use an approved substitute tool only if it has the necessary capabilities and the same resources are allowed to enter it.
4. If no suitable substitute exists, pause work and record the exact next step. After restoration, resume from the last confirmed result.

## Safety boundaries

- A limit or outage is not a reason to input work, client, or personal data into an unapproved personal tool.
- A new account, free tier, or different provider may have different data, retention, and training policies.
- Switching between models can change format, accuracy, and available tools. Compare the first output after return with the last confirmed point.
- Do not resend an action that may have occurred before the error until you verify its status. This especially applies to system writes, message sending, purchases, or deletions.

## Returning to the card

After service restoration or switching to a suitable environment:

1. load the return point,
2. show the AI the last confirmed result,
3. request only the nearest next step,
4. complete the original card without repeating finished work,
5. if the tool or model changed, note any difference that may have affected the outcome.

## Record keeping

This mechanic does not create a separate fulfillment. It can only add a return point, chosen path, and any difference after a tool change to the main card’s result.
