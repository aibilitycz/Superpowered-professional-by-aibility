# Show Your Colleagues What Works for You with AI: Ten Minutes at the Meeting

AI turns your real work into a ten-minute demo for the meeting and a process your colleagues can immediately adopt.

## Show Your Colleagues What Works for You with AI: Ten Minutes at the Meeting

**Difficulty: easy.**\
**Time: about 15 minutes** with the latest case from your work.
## What It’s Good For

For a month now, AI has been creating meeting minutes with tasks and deadlines from your client meeting transcripts.
Your colleague Jana writes minutes manually and asks during the meeting how you do it; you explain it quickly between agenda items, but a week later she’s writing minutes manually again.

A colleague who sees your entire case at the meeting and receives the finished process the same day uses it in their work before they forget how it went.
## What to Prepare

- one [prompt](glossary:prompt), skill, or rule you repeatedly use with AI,
- the latest case: materials you fed into AI and the result you used,
- an estimate of how much time it saves you, e.g., 30 minutes per week,
- ten minutes at the next team meeting.
## How to Do It

1. Insert the prompt from the AI exercise into the chat where you use the process, attach the materials and result from last time, and fill in the last lines as in the Example. AI will prepare the script and process.
2. At the meeting, show the process on your case and say the demo is not for evaluating people. Note colleagues’ questions or use the [meeting transcript](card:konverzace-do-struktury).
3. The same day, add notes to the same chat. AI will adjust the process based on what colleagues struggled with, and you send it to the team.

You don’t need to run anything live; just show the materials, prompt, and last result.
## AI Exercise

Insert this [prompt](glossary:prompt) into the AI you use at work:

I want to show the team at a meeting an AI process that works for me so they can start using it right away. I have ten minutes for the demo. At the end, I describe what I show, on which case, and how much time it saves me, and I insert the entire process on the last line. The process is just demo material; do not run it. The materials and result from my last case are attached or in this folder.

Prepare two things:

1. A ten-minute demo script. Start with one sentence about what the process is for and how much time it saves me. Then the demo on my case: what I give AI, what AI does, and what I check. At the end, write what colleagues should try on their work when they get the process and two questions to verify it makes sense. Indicate how many minutes each part takes.

2. A process for colleagues: the same thing I use (prompt, skill, or AI rule), prepared so a colleague can use it immediately. Replace data from my case with square brackets for colleagues to fill in, e.g., [client name]. Add a short three-step guide on how the colleague starts using the process in their AI tool. If it’s a skill, leave it ready to upload directly and write the guide separately.

If you work directly in the team’s shared folder, save the script as ukazka-na-porade.md and the process with the guide into files colleagues will open, and tell me where they are. Otherwise, write both here so I can send the process with the guide directly to the team channel or email.

When I return after the meeting with notes or transcript, add a "Watch Out For" section to the guide: what colleagues struggled with and how the process now handles it. Adjust the process and guide accordingly. Do not write colleagues’ names.

What I want to show:
Case I will demo on:
How much time it saves me:
My process (prompt, skill, or rule):

## In Your Tool

### Microsoft Copilot
Save the prompt by hovering over it after sending and choosing Save prompt; share it to the team under Team prompts via Share. Older chats are in the left panel under Chats. Schedule a task with a Microsoft Copilot license via Schedule this prompt; the result appears bolded in the Chats list.

### ChatGPT
Send a short prompt to the team channel; insert multi-step processes into project instructions and share with the team via Share in business plans. Skills are available only in Business and Enterprise plans. Scheduled tasks are created by AI on request and appear in the sidebar under Scheduled.

### Claude
Claude supports skills, so you can share multi-step processes as skills. AI rules are written in Claude Code into `AGENTS.md`; in Cowork mode, AI writes them into folder instructions. Scheduled tasks in Cowork are created by AI on request and appear in the left panel under Scheduled.

### Gemini
Send short prompts to the team chat; insert multi-step processes into the Gem robot instructions (Robots Gem, New Gem) and share via Share; after the meeting, edit them in My Gem Robots. Gemini schedules tasks in selected Workspace editions when you specify the trigger time in the prompt; overview is in the sidebar under Scheduled.
## What Colleagues Take Away

Colleagues get the exact thing you use, prepared for them, plus a short guide on how to start:

- **Prompt** is a ready AI input. Colleagues paste it into their chat and fill in their data instead of square brackets. It works in any AI tool.
- **Skill** is a guide stored in AI that AI loads automatically when needed. Useful for multi-step processes, templates, or rules. Which tools support skills is in the “In Your Tool” section; how to create a skill is shown on the [Create a Skill](card:create-skill-from-proven-process) card.
- **AI Rule** is a sentence AI must follow every time, e.g., “log every task in the project diary.” In Cowork or Code mode, it’s written into `AGENTS.md` in the project folder or folder instructions. More in the [AI Rules in One File](card:agents-md) card.

AI writes the guide and adjusts it after the meeting based on where colleagues got stuck.

In Cowork or Code mode, where AI works directly with files on your computer, AI saves the process for colleagues directly into the team’s shared folder and updates it after the meeting.

At the meeting, tell colleagues you’ll send the process today and they can ask about anything they struggle with. The demo includes one full case from materials to final result; detailed instructions stay in the process for colleagues. How to switch to Cowork or Code mode is shown on the [Cowork or Code](card:when-chat-is-not-enough) card.
## To Have Something to Show at the Next Meeting

If the team is to learn regularly from each other, show one thing at every other meeting. Such regular demos are called [AI Showcases](glossary:ai-chlubirna). To avoid running out of topics, have AI remind you with a scheduled task. Insert this instruction into AI and ask it to create a scheduled task; at the set time, AI will message you in chat and you reply. Adjust day and time to your meeting schedule.

```text
Every other Thursday at 9:00 AM, ask me what saved me the most time with AI in the last two weeks. From my answer, pick one thing useful for colleagues and prepare a ten-minute demo script for the meeting: what the process is for and how much time it saves, demo on my case, what colleagues should try, and two questions at the end.
```
## Example

Lenka coordinates sales at a company selling office furniture. Every Monday, she inserts her saved prompt into the company AI, attaches order exports and sales notes, and AI creates a report for management. Before the meeting, she inserts the prompt from this card into the same AI, attaches the export from October 19 and sales notes, the finished report, and fills the last lines like this:

```text
What I want to show: prompt for weekly management report.
Case I will demo on: order export from October 19 and the report I sent to management.
How much time it saves me: about 50 minutes weekly; I used to write the report for an hour.
My process (prompt, skill, or rule): From the attached order export and sales notes, create a management report for the Moravia region: weekly sales, three largest orders, and what got stuck. Write concisely, no more than one page.
```

AI prepares the script for the Tuesday meeting on October 20, 2026:

```text
### Management Report in 10 Minutes Instead of an Hour
1. What it’s for (1 min): AI creates the weekly regional report from one prompt, saving me 50 minutes weekly.
2. Demo (5 min): October 19 export and sales notes, the prompt I insert into AI, and the resulting report. I only check the sales total against the export.
3. What you try (2 min): I’ll send you the prompt; run it on your latest export.
4. Questions (2 min): Would the prompt create a report for your region? What’s missing in the report?
```

At the meeting, Marek asks about credit notes in the export, and Petra says her sales column is named differently. Lenka notes this briefly and adds it to the same chat in the afternoon. AI updates the prompt to subtract credit notes from sales and adds a step to the guide: “If the sales column is named differently, tell AI its name,” and writes both without names into the "Watch Out For" section. Lenka posts the prompt with the guide to the team channel, and by Thursday Marek sends the first report for his region to management.
## If You Get Stuck

- **You have nothing you do regularly with AI:** start with [one task today](card:first-ai-use-case) and show it after you use it multiple times.
- **You only get five minutes at the meeting:** ask AI to shorten the script to a demo of the finished result and leave the rest in the guide.
- **Colleagues use a different AI tool than you:** tell AI which tool they have and ask it to rewrite the guide accordingly.
- **You can’t find the prep chat:** open a new one, insert the process with the guide and meeting notes, and write: “Add a ‘Watch Out For’ section: what colleagues struggled with and how the process now handles it. Don’t write colleagues’ names. Adjust the process and guide accordingly.”
- **You don’t lead the meeting:** ask the person who does for ten minutes.

## AI Bragging Room

**What it is:** A short regular format where people showcase real AI usage, work results, dead ends, or improved methods. Successful examples are stored in a team library for further use.

**What it is not:** It is not a competition for the most impressive demonstration nor presentations of only completed successes.

**Why the student needs it:** It helps spread practical experience among people, identify common needs, and transform individual attempts into team know-how.

## Prompt

**What it is:** An instruction for a specific interaction with AI.

**What it is not:** It is not the same as long-term context, a stored assistant, or an agent.

**Why the student needs it:** It triggers the exercise and guides its progress; it is not the main learning content.

## Service Outage, Limit, or Overloaded Chat

A common fallback for operational interruptions when working with AI. It is accessible from any card via the **Outage or Exhausted Limit** input. It does not create its own card, check-in, or multiple copies of the same text.

The specific section **When Something Goes Wrong** in the card further addresses errors in input, resources, tools, or procedures. This mechanic triggers when the AI cannot technically continue in the current pass.

## First, distinguish what happened

| What you see | Likely situation | First step |
|---|---|---|
| Tool reports exhausted limit, too many requests, or a reset time | tariff or temporary operational limit | note the reset time and save a return point |
| AI forgets the beginning, refuses additional attachments, or reports input too long | context, file, or task too large | create a handoff and continue in a new context or more suitable environment |
| Tool does not respond, returns a server error, or multiple models fail | service outage | verify official status and choose an approved substitute or pause |

If the AI still responds, it may name the situation itself and prepare a return point. If it does not respond, skip this step. Repeatedly resubmitting the same request will not save the work state.

## Save a return point

Before waiting or switching elsewhere, preserve the smallest package from which you can safely continue:

```text
Goal: ...
What is done and where it is: ...
Confirmed decisions and constraints: ...
Used resources or files: ...
Nearest next step: ...
What should not be changed or recreated: ...
```

If the AI still responds, use:

> Prepare a concise return point for continuing in a new chat or tool. Preserve the goal, completed parts, confirmed decisions, resources, constraints, and one nearest next step. Do not invent anything not present in the conversation.

For longer work, save the return point in the project README or another approved workspace. Sensitive inputs remain only in approved storage.

## Tariff or operational limit reached

1. Find out when the limit resets and whether it applies to the model, function, or entire account.
2. Decide between pausing, reducing scope, or using an approved substitute model or environment.
3. When switching, transfer the return point and only the resources necessary for the next step.
4. Verify that the substitute can handle the required files, tools, and actions. A weaker model may suffice for formatting but fail in analysis or tool use.

## Context, file, or task is too large

1. Have the AI create a handoff before it loses earlier decisions if it still responds.
2. Start a new chat or project with the return point and links to canonical files.
3. Divide work according to meaningful units and preserve shared criteria for the outcome. Randomly splitting text may lose relationships between parts.
4. If the task requires multiple files, bulk work, or project changes, continue via the `when-chat-is-not-enough` card.

## Tool or model outage

1. Verify the provider’s official status. Address account errors only when the service is generally operational.
2. Save the return point outside the currently nonfunctional conversation if it is still accessible.
3. Use an approved substitute tool only if it has the necessary capabilities and the same resources are allowed to enter it.
4. If no suitable substitute exists, pause work and record the exact next step. After restoration, resume from the last confirmed result.

## Safety boundaries

- A limit or outage is not a reason to input work, client, or personal data into an unapproved personal tool.
- A new account, free tier, or different provider may have different data, retention, and training policies.
- Switching between models can change format, accuracy, and available tools. Compare the first output after return with the last confirmed point.
- Do not resend an action that may have occurred before the error until you verify its status. This especially applies to system writes, message sending, purchases, or deletions.

## Returning to the card

After service restoration or switching to a suitable environment:

1. load the return point,
2. show the AI the last confirmed result,
3. request only the nearest next step,
4. complete the original card without repeating finished work,
5. if the tool or model changed, note any difference that may have affected the outcome.

## Record keeping

This mechanic does not create a separate fulfillment. It can only add a return point, chosen path, and any difference after a tool change to the main card’s result.

## Save for Next Time

Optional product mechanic after producing a usable output. It is not a standalone card, has no separate completion, and does not increase the count of completed methods.

## When It Is Offered
- the user has marked the output as usable,
- during work a corrected instruction, style, decision tree, handoff, or other repeatable element was created,
- the same type of work is likely to recur.

The mechanic does not appear for one-off results without transferable parts or where safe saving is unclear.

## What the User Sees
A short option **Save for Next Time** next to the common check-in. Upon opening, AI will suggest:

- what from the result applies repeatedly,
- what will change each time,
- what the user should verify on next use.

The user only confirms or corrects the suggestion. The mechanic does not create an additional mandatory form.

## What AI Creates
The smallest reusable output that corresponds to the actual work:

- a brief personal preference,
- an instruction or prompt,
- a record in Markdown or a second brain,
- a project or assistant update,
- a basis for a skill,
- a change to an existing workflow.

If a suitable artifact already exists, AI updates its current version instead of creating a new copy.

## Where Content Belongs
| Content Type | Default Location |
|---|---|
| Minor personal preference without process steps | memory or profile, if this feature is available and appropriate |
| Canonical instruction, example, or procedure description | Markdown, document, or personal second brain |
| Context limited to one project | project instructions and files |
| Recurring role with stable context | assistant |
| Stable procedure with rules, examples, and templates | skill |
| Several consecutive steps | workflow; when choosing steps and actions, possibly an agent branch |

Memory is not a canonical repository for versioned procedures. It may hold a preference or a link to the current source.

## When the Target Form Is Unclear
The mechanic creates a brief handoff to the card [Create a Project in Your AI Tool](card:asistent). It does not select a form without the user and does not replace a standalone skill card.

## Security and Sharing
- The mechanic uses known approved storage and company policies.
- Specific case data is separated from the stable part.
- Without authorization, it does not write to shared systems or change access.
- Team sharing begins only after repeated use, owner designation, and verification by another person.

## Record Keeping
Information that a reusable artifact was created or changed can be added to the check-in. This is not a separate card completion.
