# State What AI Helped You With: Client Sentence According to Company Rules

AI helps you find out what the company rules say about AI and adds a truthful sentence to the email or report about what AI assisted with.

## State What AI Helped You With: Client Sentence According to Company Rules

**Difficulty: easy.**\
**Time: about 15 minutes** with an email or document for the client.
## When It’s Useful

On Thursday, you send the client a monthly project report whose first draft was written by AI from your notes. You’re unsure if the company allows this and worry that telling the client might make it seem like you did a rushed job.

You are responsible for the text regardless of who wrote it; if you know the company rules and add what AI helped with and what you verified, the client sees that you reviewed the work.
## What to Prepare

- An email or document you are sending to the client or outside the company this week,
- The AI policy or client contract if you have them; if not, that’s okay,
- The AI approved by your company for work.
## How to Proceed

1. Insert the prompt and answer AI about who the text is for and exactly what AI helped with. For anything you don’t know, AI will prepare a question for your manager; send it and continue. Insert the text only when AI asks for it.
2. Verify the three riskiest parts AI identifies and read the entire text. These might be a promised deadline or an unsupported figure. Then tell AI what you verified and what you changed.
3. Confirm or edit the sentence AI suggests and specify where to place it. AI will save this instruction for future chats and suggest the sentence automatically next time.

Only include in the sentence what you have actually verified.
## AI Exercise

Insert this [prompt](glossary:prompt) into the AI you use at work:

Help me prepare an email or document for sending that AI contributed to.

First, ask me who the text is for, exactly what AI helped with, and whether my company or client has AI rules, such as a policy or contract clause. Ask all questions at once and wait for answers. If I attach the policy or contract or you have access to company documents, find what applies to texts sent outside the company and quote it. Do not infer legal obligations. If you don’t find anything in the rules, write a short question for my manager that I can send directly, then continue. Prepare the sentence anyway; I will add it after the manager replies. If the rules prohibit me from inserting the text into you, tell me before asking for it.

Then ask me for the text or document name if you work with a folder on the computer. List up to three riskiest parts, such as a deadline promised in my name, unsupported claims, or estimates. For each, say where it is in the text. Remind me to read the entire text and wait until I write what I verified and changed.

Then write one sentence about what AI did and what I did, for example, “AI prepared the research and first draft; I verified the numbers.” Include only what I said I verified and adapt it to the recipient and rules. Specify exactly where to place it, e.g., under the table or after the last paragraph of the email. If the sentence does not belong anywhere according to rules and situation, say so and explain why.

Finally, save the instruction for next time: “For texts I send to clients or outside the company, before sending, list the riskiest parts and suggest a sentence about what AI did and what I did. These rules apply: [brief summary of rules you found].” If you found no rules, write instead “I don’t know the company rules yet; I will send the sentence to the client only with manager approval.” Save it to memory. If you have no memory, write it so I can insert it into my Custom Instructions and tell me where to find them in settings.

## In Your Tool

### Microsoft Copilot
Attach the AI policy in Copilot Chat using the + button from OneDrive or SharePoint. Copilot saves the instruction to memory when you write “remember this.” You can also add it to Custom Instructions for all chats: three dots top right, Settings and more, Chat settings, Customization, Custom Instructions.

### ChatGPT
Attach the policy via + next to the message field. ChatGPT saves the instruction to memory (Settings, Personalization, Memory), and if you don’t have memory, insert it in Custom Instructions there. In the Czech version, these items are in the same place but in Czech.

### Claude
Attach the policy with the + button in chat. Claude saves the instruction to memory (Settings, Memory), which must be enabled by the organization owner in corporate plans; without it, insert the instruction in Settings under Instructions for Claude. In Cowork, where AI works with files in an open folder, just write the document name instead of the text.

### Gemini
Attach the policy from Google Drive via Add files. Work accounts have no memory or custom instructions, so save the instruction as a skill: write “Save this as a skill” and find it in Customize, Skills. Enabled skills are used automatically by Gemini.
## Where the AI Sentence Belongs

Where to place the sentence depends on how much work AI did and what the recipient expects from you.

- **If AI prepared a substantial part** of research, analysis, or an offer that the client considers your expert work, place the sentence under the report or after the last paragraph of the email. If you don’t know the company rules, add the sentence after the manager replies; send the text without it if it goes out earlier.
- **If AI only corrected typos or shortened your text,** usually you don’t write the sentence unless company or client rules explicitly require it.
- **In regulated fields,** where work is overseen by authorities or professional chambers, AI disclosure is often mandatory, so company policies and industry rules apply.

The sentence might say, for example, “AI summarized the materials and wrote the first draft; I verified conclusions and deadlines,” or “AI prepared the translation; I checked the technical terms.” If someone asks whether you used AI, answer honestly even if you didn’t write the sentence.
## Example

Martina prepares summaries for corporate clients at a real estate agency. On Thursday, October 22, she sends Horák Logistics a comparison of warehouse rents around Brno; AI found offers on real estate servers and wrote the first draft of the commentary.

The contract with Horák Logistics says nothing about AI, but since September the agency has AI guidelines on the intranet. Martina attached them, and AI found clause 4: outputs for clients must disclose when AI prepared a substantial part. Among the three riskiest parts was a promise that Martina would arrange all warehouse viewings by Friday. AI added that promise itself, and Martina deleted it because owners arrange viewings. She verified prices and areas in listings and inserted the sentence AI suggested under the table:

```text
AI found warehouse offers and wrote the first draft of the commentary. I verified prices and areas in listings as of October 20, 2026.
```

Martina does not add the sentence to the weekly report for sales colleagues because the guidelines require it only for client outputs. She saved the instruction in AI memory because the guidelines apply to all clients.
## If You Get Stuck

- **Manager doesn’t reply before sending:** send the text without the sentence and add the prepared sentence after the reply. Then insert the reply into AI with “Adjust your instruction accordingly.” If the client asks whether you used AI, answer honestly.
- **Client contract restricts AI:** send the text only after clarifying with the contact person. AI will prepare a question for you from the contract quote.
- **Sentence sounds like an apology:** write “Shorten it to one factual sentence: what AI did and what I did.”
- **New chat doesn’t know the instruction:** memory is off or disabled by admin in corporate account. Insert the instruction into [Custom Instructions](card:custom-instructions); AI will use it only for texts sent outside the company, so it won’t affect other chats. If it applies only to one client, put it into the [AI project instructions](card:asistent) for that client.
## What Will Be Ready

The text you are sending this week has been fully read and its riskiest parts reviewed. You know what AI rules apply in your company and with the client or have sent a question about them. The AI sentence is in the text where it belongs or awaits the manager’s reply; or you know why you are not writing it this time. AI has saved the instruction to suggest it automatically next time.
## What Next

If the text relies on numbers from source materials, [verify them with AI against the sources](card:kontrola-vysledku). To ensure the whole team discloses AI consistently, write [team AI rules](card:pravidla-pro-tym) and add when you disclose AI in texts sent outside.

## Service Outage, Limit, or Overloaded Chat

A common fallback for operational interruptions when working with AI. It is accessible from any card via the **Outage or Exhausted Limit** input. It does not create its own card, check-in, or multiple copies of the same text.

The specific section **When Something Goes Wrong** in the card further addresses errors in input, resources, tools, or procedures. This mechanic triggers when the AI cannot technically continue in the current pass.

## First, distinguish what happened

| What you see | Likely situation | First step |
|---|---|---|
| Tool reports exhausted limit, too many requests, or a reset time | tariff or temporary operational limit | note the reset time and save a return point |
| AI forgets the beginning, refuses additional attachments, or reports input too long | context, file, or task too large | create a handoff and continue in a new context or more suitable environment |
| Tool does not respond, returns a server error, or multiple models fail | service outage | verify official status and choose an approved substitute or pause |

If the AI still responds, it may name the situation itself and prepare a return point. If it does not respond, skip this step. Repeatedly resubmitting the same request will not save the work state.

## Save a return point

Before waiting or switching elsewhere, preserve the smallest package from which you can safely continue:

```text
Goal: ...
What is done and where it is: ...
Confirmed decisions and constraints: ...
Used resources or files: ...
Nearest next step: ...
What should not be changed or recreated: ...
```

If the AI still responds, use:

> Prepare a concise return point for continuing in a new chat or tool. Preserve the goal, completed parts, confirmed decisions, resources, constraints, and one nearest next step. Do not invent anything not present in the conversation.

For longer work, save the return point in the project README or another approved workspace. Sensitive inputs remain only in approved storage.

## Tariff or operational limit reached

1. Find out when the limit resets and whether it applies to the model, function, or entire account.
2. Decide between pausing, reducing scope, or using an approved substitute model or environment.
3. When switching, transfer the return point and only the resources necessary for the next step.
4. Verify that the substitute can handle the required files, tools, and actions. A weaker model may suffice for formatting but fail in analysis or tool use.

## Context, file, or task is too large

1. Have the AI create a handoff before it loses earlier decisions if it still responds.
2. Start a new chat or project with the return point and links to canonical files.
3. Divide work according to meaningful units and preserve shared criteria for the outcome. Randomly splitting text may lose relationships between parts.
4. If the task requires multiple files, bulk work, or project changes, continue via the `when-chat-is-not-enough` card.

## Tool or model outage

1. Verify the provider’s official status. Address account errors only when the service is generally operational.
2. Save the return point outside the currently nonfunctional conversation if it is still accessible.
3. Use an approved substitute tool only if it has the necessary capabilities and the same resources are allowed to enter it.
4. If no suitable substitute exists, pause work and record the exact next step. After restoration, resume from the last confirmed result.

## Safety boundaries

- A limit or outage is not a reason to input work, client, or personal data into an unapproved personal tool.
- A new account, free tier, or different provider may have different data, retention, and training policies.
- Switching between models can change format, accuracy, and available tools. Compare the first output after return with the last confirmed point.
- Do not resend an action that may have occurred before the error until you verify its status. This especially applies to system writes, message sending, purchases, or deletions.

## Returning to the card

After service restoration or switching to a suitable environment:

1. load the return point,
2. show the AI the last confirmed result,
3. request only the nearest next step,
4. complete the original card without repeating finished work,
5. if the tool or model changed, note any difference that may have affected the outcome.

## Record keeping

This mechanic does not create a separate fulfillment. It can only add a return point, chosen path, and any difference after a tool change to the main card’s result.
